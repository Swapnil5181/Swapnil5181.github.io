Project 01 · Electrothermal

Multiphysics Modeling of Composites

Coupled current flow and Joule heating in angled SiC/Cu composite conductors

When a conductor is made of a metal matrix reinforced with ceramic fibres, its electrical and thermal conductivities are no longer the same in every direction. Tilt the fibres and the conductivity becomes a full tensor, so current no longer flows straight down the strip and heat no longer spreads evenly.

I solve the two fields together: charge conservation for the electric potential, and heat conduction with the Joule heat it produces as the source. Both conductivities depend on temperature, so the solver iterates between the two problems until they agree. The study runs through five conductor shapes of increasing corner complexity (I, L, Z, U and S lines), sweeping fibre angle and fibre volume fraction.

The re-entrant corners are the hard part numerically. Current crowds there sharply, so the boundary stencils need special treatment at each corner orientation, and the finite-difference results are checked against an independent finite-element model that shares no code with it.

Physics
∇·(σ∇V) = 0 coupled to ∇·(κ∇T) + qJ = losses, with σ(T), κ(T) as rotated tensors
Material
SiC fibres in a copper matrix, fibre angle θ from 0° to 180°, volume fraction vf
Shapes
I, L, Z, U, S lines; 5 mm limb width, equal 500 mm² planform
Numerics
Finite differences with dedicated re-entrant corner stencils; Q4 finite elements as an independent check
Code
MATLAB and Python (NumPy, SciPy sparse, Numba)
Shape
Fibre angle

Temperature rise ΔT, 0 to 25 K on a common scale · white lines follow the current

The corner that carries the most crowded current is not the hottest place in the conductor. Conduction smooths a sharp, local heat source, so the peak temperature settles along the limbs instead, and the corner geometry matters more than how many corners there are.

Project 02 · Structural

Structural Analysis of Composites

A displacement-potential finite-difference solver for angle-ply laminae

This solver computes the full stress and displacement field in a fibre-reinforced plate with the fibres at any angle. Instead of solving for two displacement components, it uses a single displacement potential ψ that satisfies one equilibrium equation identically. The other becomes one fourth-order governing equation, discretised with a 21-point stencil.

Mixed boundary conditions are the reason for this approach. Each edge can carry a prescribed stress, a prescribed displacement, or both, and every one of them is written in terms of ψ through ghost-node equations. Once ψ is known, displacements and all three stress components follow from its derivatives at every node.

The test case is a glass/epoxy plate clamped at both ends and pressed on its top face. Rotating the fibres breaks the left/right symmetry of the response, which the contours below make easy to see.

Method
Displacement-potential formulation, after Ahmed, Hossain & Uddin (2005)
Equation
b₁ψ,xxxx + b₂ψ,xxxy + b₃ψ,xxyy + b₄ψ,xyyy + b₅ψ,yyyy = 0
Material
Glass/epoxy lamina, E₁ = 38.6 GPa, E₂ = 8.27 GPa, G₁₂ = 4.14 GPa
Problem
3 : 1 plate, both ends clamped, 5 MPa on the top face, free bottom face
Grid
241 × 121 nodes plus ghost layer, sparse direct solve
Code
MATLAB

Part two

AIAA Design/Build/Fly 2026

Role · Simulation and Testing Team Leader

17thof 175 teams worldwide for the design report

I led simulation and testing for the Military Institute of Science and Technology team's entry, HeisenBird: a 60-inch span, low-wing tractor monoplane built for passenger and cargo transport and banner towing under the competition's 100 Wh energy limit.

The four studies below are my work for the design report: winglet aerodynamics, the wingtip vortex comparison, finite-element analysis of the main landing gear, and the scoring sensitivity analysis that set the aircraft's mission targets.

Winglet aerodynamic analysis

ANSYS Fluentk–ω SST

A forward wingtip fence was simulated to see what it does to flow near the tip. The domain extended 5 chords upstream, 10 chords downstream and 5 chords laterally, and the angle of attack was changed by rotating the freestream velocity while the geometry stayed fixed. The velocity and pressure fields show the winglet restricting spanwise flow at the tip, which weakens the tip vortex and cuts induced drag.

Wingtip vortex analysis

CFDFlow trajectories

Without a winglet, spanwise flow rolls around the tip into a coherent vortex that persists downstream. With the winglet fitted, the vortex core is weaker and more diffuse, with less wake rotation. That is lower induced drag and better lift efficiency without adding span, which the rules cap at 5 ft. The winglet also adds a small amount of directional stability, and it went into the final design on the strength of this comparison.

FEA of the main landing gear

ANSYS Static Structural

The carbon-fibre main gear was checked for a hard landing at 2.5 g on the maximum take-off weight. The upper attachment was fixed to represent the fuselage joint and the landing load was applied at the wheel mounts. Peak von Mises stress sits at the upper bend, where the bending moment is largest, at 109 MPa (15.8 ksi). Against a conservative 500 MPa strength for carbon fibre, that is a factor of safety of about 4.6, and the gear absorbs the landing through elastic deflection of its legs.

Scoring sensitivity analysis

MATLABParametric modelDesign-space search

A parametric scoring model in MATLAB computed the total competition score from airframe, propulsion and mission variables, with the 2026 scoring constants built in. Lap time came out as the strongest driver for both flight missions, with ground-mission time second. More passengers or a longer banner raise the score only up to a point, after which their weight and drag slow the laps enough to lose points. An energy-constrained search over thousands of mission combinations then located the best balance, which set the targets of 24 passengers and 8 cargo units for Mission 2 and a 60-inch banner for Mission 3.
